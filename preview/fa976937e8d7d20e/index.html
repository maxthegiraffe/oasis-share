Grateful Giraffes · The WhatsApp Playbook

Fewer pings, better conversations.

The communities that feel alive on WhatsApp all do the same few things. Here they are, and how we could use them for the Giraffes.

1 · Structure

One Community, a few clear groups

People mute a group when they can't tell what it's for.

WhatsApp Communities let you put many groups under one roof, with one Announcements group at the top that everyone gets. The best run ones keep it small and obvious.

  • Give every group one job

    A name and a one line description that says what belongs there. Think by place, by event or by interest, plus one general chat.

  • Start with fewer groups than you think

    A quiet room feels empty. Open a new group only when a topic keeps taking over another one, or when members ask for it.

  • Close groups that have done their job

    An event group can wind down a week after the event. Fewer dead rooms means people trust the live ones.

  • Mind the ceiling

    A single WhatsApp group tops out at 1,024 people. Long before that, a big group gets noisy, so split by city or interest early.

2 · Announcements vs chat

Announcements are not chat

Mixing them is the fastest way to get the whole community muted.

Announcements group

Only admins post. Members can react, reply to admins and vote in polls.

Keep it rare and worth opening: what's happening, when, and one link. If everything is an announcement, nothing is.

Chat groups

Where members talk to each other: questions, photos after a gathering, rides, recommendations.

Admins host here instead of broadcast: ask a question, answer, thank people.

For people who aren't members yet, a WhatsApp Channel is a one way feed anyone can follow, so it can carry the public news without opening our groups to everyone.

3 · Rules and onboarding

Rules and a real welcome

WhatsApp's own guide for admins says the communities that last set clear rules from day one.

Keep them short enough to read on a phone. Put them in the Community description, pin them in Announcements, and repeat them now and then. A starting point for us:

  1. Be kind. We're here to feel more grateful, not less.
  2. Post in the group that fits. Not sure? Use the general chat.
  3. Use reply so threads stay easy to follow.
  4. No selling or promotion without asking an admin first.
  5. What's shared here stays here.

  • Turn on member approval

    With "Approve new participants" on, even an invite link sends people to an admin first. It keeps the room the room.

  • Welcome each new person by name

    A short personal note from an admin, the rules, and which group to join first. WhatsApp's guide calls a personal welcome one of the most encouraging things an admin can do.

  • Give them an easy first post

    One prompt to introduce themselves, like where they're from and what they're grateful for this week. The first message is the hardest one.

4 · Moderation

Moderation that feels like hosting

Members expect the rules to be kept, the same way, every time.

  • A small admin team, not one person

    Two or three hosts per group, sharing the load so nobody burns out and someone is always around.

  • Remove harmful posts right away

    Then talk to the person privately first. If it happens again, remove them.

  • Say what happened, briefly

    After a removal, restate the rule in Announcements so everyone sees it's kept fairly.

  • Lock the settings

    Only admins edit group names, photos and descriptions, and only admins add new groups to the Community.

  • Catch people doing it right

    Thank the member who welcomed a newcomer or shared photos after a gathering. Praise in public shapes the room more than rules do.

5 · Cadence

A rhythm people can count on

A predictable beat beats a flood. People learn when to look.

New groups usually get a burst of energy at the start, then settle into a smaller core of regulars. Plan for the regulars. A simple week could look like this:

Start of the weekOne Announcements post: what's on this week, one link for details.
MidweekOne prompt or poll in the general chat to get members talking to each other.
Before a gatheringOne reminder in the event or city group, with where and when.
After a gatheringInvite photos and highlights in chat. This is where the warmth shows.

  • Mix the formats

    Polls, voice notes, photos and the odd scheduled group call break up a wall of text.

  • Respect the evening

    Send announcements at a set time of day, and never late at night.

6 · Alongside email

WhatsApp and email, each doing its job

WhatsApp is the tap on the shoulder. Email is the record.

Email and the Oasis

The full story: event details, the why, the links, the calendar invite. Easy to find later, and it reaches people who mute chats.

WhatsApp

The nudge and the conversation: a one line heads up that points to the full details, questions in the moment, and the photos afterward.

One simple habit holds it together: anything important lives in email or on the Oasis first, and the WhatsApp post links to it. Send them the same day, so the two never disagree.

The first 30 days

Where we could start

  1. Map what we have. List every Giraffes group, what it's for, and whether it's still alive.
  2. Pick the shape. One Community, one Announcements group, a general chat, and only the city, event or interest groups that earn a place.
  3. Write the five rules and put them in the description and pinned in Announcements.
  4. Turn on member approval and write a welcome note admins can make their own.
  5. Name the hosts for each group, two or three each.
  6. Set the weekly rhythm and pair every Announcements post with the email or Oasis page it points to.
  7. Look back after a month: which groups are talking, which are quiet, and what members ask for.
