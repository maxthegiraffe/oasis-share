Seamless · 54:01 · 124.000 BPM · A minor · engineered blends

Seamless

Sixteen tracks engineered so you cannot hear where one ends. Everything is locked to one tempo and one key - 124.000 BPM, A minor - because harmonic variety and invisible transitions are opposites, and this set chose invisible. The variety comes from instrumentation alone: felt piano, bowed double bass, harp, tanpura and sitar, hammered dulcimer, kalimba, cimbalom, bouzouki, fretless bass, cor anglais, vibraphone, saz, glass armonica, tabla, bandoneon, duduk - one lead instrument each, over the same sub-heavy dub-house bed. Three things beyond ordinary beatmatching: every track is pitch-corrected to a common tonal center (they arrived spread across 6.6 cents, which beats audibly when two of them overlap); every track is pulled toward a shared broad spectral curve; and the blends are EQ-split - the sub hands over on a single downbeat while the mids and highs cross over sixteen bars, so there is only ever one bassline playing. The running order was not chosen by taste. Every one of the 240 possible tail-to-head pairings was measured and the sequence solved to minimise the worst seam, which cut it by 2.4x. Verified by cold-splicing each pair with no crossfade at all: 11 of 15 transitions are indistinguishable from the natural variation inside the tracks themselves.

Hebrew House Deep · 61:05 · 124 BPM · A minor

Hebrew House Deep

Seventeen tracks reverse-engineered from one song - Petach HaLev, the opener of the Hebrew House set. Measured against its neighbours it turned out to be twice as bass-heavy, far darker, and almost perfectly flat - it never builds and never drops. So every track here is held to that fingerprint: 124 BPM, A minor, sub-forward, no drops. What changes is the instrument carrying the hook - Rhodes, Hammond organ, muted trumpet, santur, mbira, acid 303, upright bass, cello, clarinet, oud, a chopped Hebrew vocal. The Hebrew is murmured in tape delay rather than sung, the way the original does it. Sequenced as a plateau, not an arc - the body holds inside 1.2 dB and the journey is carried by texture instead of volume, because flatness is the thing that makes the source track work. Tempo-locked to 124.000, beat-aligned, crossfaded in whole bars - 16 at the open and the landing, 8 through the body.
