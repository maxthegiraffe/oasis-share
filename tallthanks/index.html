More than manners

Saying thank you is manners. Noticing is gratitude.

Most of us grew up being told to say thank you. In one 10-day diary study of 100 parents of 6 to 9 year olds, 85% prompted a thank-you or good manners. Only 22% asked why someone had given the gift.1

Researchers in the Raising Grateful Children project at UNC Chapel Hill describe gratitude in four parts: noticing what we've been given, thinking about who made it possible, feeling the warmth of their kindness, and doing something to show it.2 A thank-you on its own is good manners. Gratitude notices, thinks and feels first.

    Giraffe facts kids love

    One more fact before lights out.

    Real giraffe fact

    No. 1

    Seven, like you

    A giraffe has the same number of neck bones as you: seven! Each one is just much, much longer.

    Giraffes have seven neck vertebrae, the same number as humans, and each can be over 25 cm (10 in) long.

    San Diego Zoo

    Real giraffe fact

    No. 2

    A blue-purple tongue

    A giraffe's tongue is longer than your arm from elbow to hand, and it's dark blue-purple!

    The tongue is about 45 to 50 cm (17 to 19 in) long and can wrap around branches and work between thorns. The front is blue-black to purple. The base, which stays inside the mouth, is pink.

    Giraffe Conservation Foundation

    Real giraffe fact

    No. 3

    Born with a tumble

    When a baby giraffe is born, it takes a big tumble to the ground, then wobbles up onto its feet in about an hour.

    Giraffe mothers give birth standing up. The newborn drops about 2 m (6.5 ft) to the ground and is usually standing within about an hour.

    Giraffe Conservation Foundation

    Real giraffe fact

    No. 4

    Born tall

    A giraffe mommy carries her baby for more than a year, and her baby is born taller than most grown-ups!

    Pregnancy lasts about 14 to 15 months. Newborns are about 1.8 m (6 ft) tall, and calves nearly double their height in their first year.

    Giraffe Conservation Foundation

    Real giraffe fact

    No. 5

    A big, strong heart

    A giraffe's heart is big and strong. It weighs about as much as a two-year-old child!

    An adult giraffe heart weighs about 11 kg (24 to 25 lb) and is about 60 cm (2 ft) long, with a very muscular left ventricle.

    San Diego Zoo

    Real giraffe fact

    No. 6

    A shy cousin

    A giraffe's closest cousin is the okapi, a shy forest animal with stripy legs like a zebra!

    The okapi, a zebra-striped forest animal of the Democratic Republic of Congo, is the giraffe's closest living relative. The two are the only living members of the family Giraffidae.

    Giraffe Conservation Foundation

    For grown-ups

    What we know, and what we don't.

    We won't promise you a more grateful child. Here's what the research says, plainly.

    • Understanding varies.In one study of 263 children, understanding of gratitude at age 5 varied widely. With babies and toddlers, this is you planting seeds out loud.3
    • Grown-ups going first may matter.In a week-long study of 101 families with children aged 6 to 9, on days when a parent modeled and talked about gratitude more than usual, their child showed relatively more gratitude that same day. Parents reported both, so this is a same-day link, not proof of cause.4
    • Thanks come with time.In a 1976 study of trick-or-treaters, 11 to 16 year olds were four times more likely to say thanks for candy than 6 year olds.5
    • The bigger picture is humble.A 2016 review of 20 studies concluded that gratitude programs for young people, taken as a whole, were generally ineffective, and that more research is needed.6 Parent-training tools in this area have had modest results so far.7

    So we keep it simple. Two minutes, a giraffe, and a grown-up who goes first. We think noticing kindness together is worth doing anyway.

    Wild giraffes

    For the wild ones.

    There isn't just one kind of giraffe. There are four kinds, each living in a different part of Africa.8 Giraffes used to live in many more places. Now people are helping them come back home.9 About 140,000 giraffes live wild in Africa (GCF count, 2025), and people are working hard to help that number grow.10

    For grown-ups: researchers tell individual wild giraffe apart by their coat patterns. The Giraffe Conservation Foundation and partners monitor more than 33,000 giraffe this way.

    Learn about wild giraffes at the Giraffe Conservation Foundation
