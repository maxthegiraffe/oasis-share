    01Executive summary

    A Venice church, bought for its rooms and held for its rent

    Grateful Space Group is raising to buy, retrofit and open 132 Brooks Avenue: a 1930 church building of 9,498 square feet on a 0.34-acre corner where Abbot Kinney Boulevard meets Main Street in Venice. The building is listed at $5.9 million after two price cuts. We model a purchase at $4.0 million (the base case) and at $5.0 million.

    After a light retrofit, the whole property is leased on a 15-year triple-net master lease to Grateful Giraffes, which runs it as a third space: a members' living room and workspace by day, a hall for gatherings, talks and private events at night, with a café, a garden courtyard and a studio where founders build companies.

    Investors are paid from the rent. The mortgage is fixed and the rent rises 3% a year, so the cash left for investors grows each year. No sale is assumed. Investors also participate in the ventures that come out of the studio, which is not counted in any projection here.

    Permit path

    1 · Pre-application

    Meet LA City Planning during due diligence to confirm the use and the conditions it will need.

    2 · Conditional Use Permit

    4 · Building and fire

    Plan check for the retrofit and the assembly occupancy, then construction.

    13Grateful Space Group

    The first of many

    America is closing churches faster than it opens them. Most sit in walkable, established neighborhoods and were built for one thing: bringing people together. Grateful Space Group buys them, keeps the bones, and turns each one into a third space run by and for its neighbors.

    4,000
    Protestant churches closed in 2024, while about 3,800 opened. Lifeway Research
    800
    Christian Science congregations left in the U.S. by 2015, down from 1,300 in 1995. Baylor Religion Watch
    617
    days 132 Brooks has been on the market. Few buyers know what to do with a sanctuary.

    What we look for

    • 5,000 to 15,000 square feet of assembly space
    • Walkable neighborhoods with strong retail streets
    • Zoning that allows assembly use, or a clear permit path to it
    • A price near land value
    • A congregation that wants its building to keep gathering people

    Buy, retrofit, lease, hold

    The rooms that make churches hard to sell (big halls, kitchens, many restrooms) are exactly what a third space needs. A light retrofit costs a fraction of building new; a long lease to a community operator fills the rooms day and night; and we hold for the rent. 132 Brooks is building No. 1.

    15Risk factors

    What could go wrong

    No contract yet

    The property is listed at $5.9M. The seller may not accept $4.0M or $5.0M, and the terms are not agreed.

    Permits

    A community center in the RD1.5 zone needs a Conditional Use Permit, and the Venice Coastal Zone needs a Coastal Development Permit. Approval can be slow, come with conditions on hours, occupancy or parking, be appealed to the California Coastal Commission, or be denied.

    Schedule and the reserve

    The reserve covers 12 months of mortgage. A longer permit or construction period uses more cash and delays distributions.

    Construction costs

    Accessibility, life safety and kitchen work in a 1930 wood building can cost more than planned. The budget carries a 10% contingency.

    One tenant, a related party

    All income comes from Grateful Giraffes, which is affiliated with the sponsor. If it cannot pay, investors' cash stops until the building is re-let.

    Operating risk

    Membership, event and café revenue are projections. Third spaces are hard: NeueHouse closed all its locations, including Venice, in September 2025.

    Financing

    No lender has committed. A converted church is a special-purpose property; the loan may be smaller, cost more, or come due before it is paid off.

    Property tax and insurance

    The religious exemption ends at sale. Taxes and insurance are paid by the tenant, which squeezes its margin if they rise.

    Site conditions

    Coastal parcels can carry liquefaction, seismic or environmental issues, and a 1930 building may draw historic review. To be checked in due diligence.

    Studio ventures

    Most early-stage companies fail. The participation may never be worth anything.

    Illiquidity

    No sale is planned and interests cannot be freely transferred. Expect to hold for many years.

    Projections

    Every figure here is an estimate. Distributions and the preferred return are not guaranteed, and you could lose your entire investment.
