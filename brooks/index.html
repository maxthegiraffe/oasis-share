The building

Built to gather people. Ready to do it again.

The Twentieth Church of Christ, Scientist has stood on the corner of Brooks and Main since 1930: a navy clapboard hall with a white-columned portico, two big halls with original wood floors, arched stained glass and a skylight, five minutes on foot from the boardwalk. The congregation is selling.

How investors get paid

A rent check that grows every year.

No sale is assumed. Investors own the building and collect the rent.

1 · The lease

Grateful Giraffes signs a long-term triple-net master lease for the whole building at $4.25 a square foot a month, about a year, rising 3% every year. The operator pays tax, insurance and upkeep.

2 · The mortgage

Rent pays the fixed mortgage of a year and about $35K of fund costs. The mortgage stays flat while the rent climbs, so the cash left over grows faster than 3%.

3 · The investors

What's left is paid out quarterly: an 8% cumulative preferred return to investors first, then 75% to investors and 25% to the sponsor.

Sanctuary Fund

Risks

What could go wrong.

Read these before you invest.

No contract yet

The building is listed at $5.9M. Price and terms are not agreed, and the seller may not accept $4M or $5M.

Permits

RD1.5 is a residential zone, so a community center needs a Conditional Use Permit, and the Venice Coastal Zone needs a Coastal Development Permit. Approval can take longer than the 12 months the reserve covers, or come with conditions.

One tenant, a related party

The rent depends on Grateful Giraffes, the sponsor's own operating company. Coverage is 1.6x in the base case; a weak operator means weak rent.

Third spaces are hard

NeueHouse Venice closed in 2025. Membership, events and café revenue are projections, not history.

A 1930 building

Accessibility, life safety and kitchen work in an old wood building can cost more than planned. The budget carries a 10% contingency.

Property tax resets

The church's religious exemption ends at sale. About a year, paid by the operator under the triple-net lease.

Debt

The model assumes a 7.0% fixed loan on about half the cost. A higher rate or a smaller loan lowers the yield or raises the raise.

Long hold, little liquidity

No sale is planned. Investors should expect to hold for many years.
